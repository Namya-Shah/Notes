# Notes
- Everyone has inputs to give, ideas to share but if we allow anxiety to get in the way of that we miss out society misses out
- We lose valuable diverse voices
- The communicators anxiety makes us feel nervous
- We need to manage our anxiety so that people pay attention to us when we speak and not get distracted
- When we start communicating in high stakes situations we feel our heart pound, we get shaky, maybe we perspire or blush. We can manage these symptoms. Take a deep breath before you start the kind of breath you would do if you’re doing yoga or TaiChi or QiGong that’ll calm you down. If you shake, gesture broadly. Big broad gestures they can really help that adrenalin go somewhere.
- Most nervous people make themselves small and tight and they hold it in and shake if you perspire a blush, hold something cold in the palm of your hand, it’ll cool you down.
- Instead of running away from your anxiety, greet your anxiety. This is me feeling nervous.
- I’m afraid I’m going to forget. A great way to manage this is to have a map. What → So what → Now what
- The valuation in judgement we feel the audience is doing on us. A great way to manage this is to redirect their attention, have them focus on something else.
- How we see our communication. There is no right way to communicate. We need to see communication as a performance as something else and I’d like to suggest we need to reframe it as a conversation. Put your attention on your audience. With these audience centric approach it makes it very conversational. Use words like you, us, and we that involves people and finally ask questions
- With a little self kindness and patience, we can learn to manage anxiety

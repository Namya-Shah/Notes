# Notes
- The **Armijo Principle** is a condition used in optimization algorithms, such as gradient descent, to ensure that a sufficient decrease in the objective function is achieved at each step. It is a part of family of techniques called [[Line-Search Methods]], which determine how far to move along the descent direction (e.g., the negative gradient) during each iteration.
- The **Armijo Principle** (or **Armijo Rule**) is a **backtracking line search method**.
The primary goal of the Armijo principle is to balance two competing objectives:
1. **Making sufficient progress** toward minimizing the objective function.
2. **Avoiding excessively large steps** that could lead to instability or divergence.
# Choosing appropriate size of $\alpha_k$
- If $\alpha_k$ is too small, convergence will be slow.
- If $\alpha_k$ is too large, the algorithm may overshoot the minimum or even diverge.
The **Armijo principle** provides a systematic way to choose $\alpha_k$ to ensure progress toward the minimum.
> [!NOTE] NOTE
> Armijo condition ensures that the step size $\alpha_k$ results in sufficient decrease in the objective function.

$$
f(x_k - \alpha_k \nabla f(x_k)) \le f(x_k)-c \cdot \alpha_k ||\nabla f(x_k)||^2
$$

where,
- $c$ is a constant in the range $(0,1)$, typically small (e.g., $c = 10^{-4}$),
- $||\nabla f(x_k)||^2$ is the squared norm of the gradient
> This inequality ensures that the reduction in the objective function $f$ is proportional to the step size $\alpha_k$ and the magnitude of the gradient
# Working of Armijo Principle
1. **Start with an initial step size** $\alpha_k$ (e.g., $\alpha_k = 1$)
2. **Check the Armijo Condition**
	- If the *condition is satisfied*, **accept $\alpha_k$** and proceed to the next iteration.
	- If *not*, **reduce $\alpha_k$** (e.g., by a factor of $\beta \in (0,1)$, such as $\beta = 0.5$) and recheck the condition.
3. **Repeat** until the condition is satisfied.
# Why the Armijo Principle Helps Gradient Descent Locate Minima
1. **Guarantees Progress**: By ensuring a sufficient decrease in the objective function, the Armijo principle guarantees that each step moves closer to the minimum.
2. **Adaptive Step Size**: It dynamically adjusts the step size based on the local behavior of the function, making it robust to variations in the curvature of the objective function.
3. **Avoids Divergence**: By preventing excessively large steps, it helps avoid overshooting the minimum or diverging.
4. **Convergence**: Under certain conditions (e.g., smoothness and convexity of $f$), the Armijo principle ensures that gradient descent converges to a local minimum.
# Limitations
- The Armijo rule may require multiple function evaluations to find an acceptable step size, which can be computationally expensive.
- It does not guarantee the optimal step size, only a sufficient one.
